Environmental Crisis Coverage

This chart tracks how often climate, biodiversity, and resource crisis keywords appear in Flemish media each week. Climate crisis coverage (blue) is consistently the highest and shows two clear peaks, one in early February and one in mid-March, before gradually declining toward June. Biodiversity (red) and resource coverage (green) follow similar patterns but at lower levels, staying relatively close to each other throughout the period. All three lines drop sharply in late May, suggesting a general decrease in environmental coverage toward the end of the period.

Subcategory Coverage

These three charts break down environmental coverage into its subcategories across Flemish channels. The climate chart shows how coverage is split between causes, consequences, and solutions. The biodiversity and resource charts follow the same logic, showing which framing each channel uses most. Together they reveal not just how much environmental coverage each channel produces, but what angle they take..

Observation

Canvas and VTM lead in climate coverage. Across all channels, climate cause coverage makes up the largest share, while solution-oriented coverage (both adaptation and attenuation) remains consistently low. This suggests Flemish media tends to frame climate in terms of problems rather than responses.

Observation

VRT1 and VTM produce the most biodiversity coverage. The observation subcategory dominates across all channels, meaning coverage tends to describe biodiversity loss rather than explain its causes or consequences. QMUSIC and STU BRU show very little biodiversity coverage overall.

Observation

Canvas, VTM, and Play lead in resource coverage. The split between observation and solutions varies more across channels than in the other subcategories, with some channels showing a higher share of solution-framed content. Radio channels again show the lowest overall levels.

Keyword Count

This chart shows how frequently each environmental keyword appears across all Flemish media in the dataset. The keywords are ranked from most to least common, giving a clear picture of which environmental terms dominate the media language. This helps identify whether coverage tends to use broad general terms or more specific climate vocabulary.

Observation

"Ecologisch" and "vergroenen" appear far more often than any other keywords, suggesting Flemish media discusses the environment in broad terms. More specific words like "klimaatverandering" and "fossiele brandstoffen" rank much lower, which may limit how clearly climate issues are explained to the public.

Key Insight

Framing decides what climate change becomes in the public mind.

Research shows that the way climate change is framed can shape public engagement and support for action.

Nisbet, M. C. (2009). Communicating Climate Change: Why Frames Matter for Public Engagement. Environment: Science and Policy for Sustainable Development, 51(2), 12–23.

Sector Analysis

This chart tracks how often different sectors appear in Flemish environmental coverage each week. The sectors range from energy and industry to agriculture, transport, and ecosystem. By following each line over time, you can see which sectors consistently attract attention and which only appear in response to specific news events.

Observation

Industry dominates in early February with a sharp spike, followed by a second peak in late February. Energy rises steadily from March onward and peaks in May, suggesting it became more prominent later in the period. Most other sectors (including agriculture, transport, and buildings) remain consistently low throughout, indicating that environmental coverage in Flemish media is largely concentrated in just two sectors.

Sector Analysis per Channel

This chart shows how each Flemish channel distributes its environmental coverage across sectors. Each bar adds up to 100%, so you can directly compare which sectors each channel emphasises relative to its own total environmental output. This reveals whether channels cover the same sectors or take noticeably different approaches.

Observation

Agriculture and energy make up the largest shares across most channels, but the balance differs. Canvas and QMUSIC dedicate a larger proportion to agriculture, while VTM and Play lean more toward energy. Resource economy takes up a consistently large share across all channels. Sectors like transport, buildings, and water appear as small slivers everywhere, suggesting these topics are structurally underrepresented in Flemish environmental coverage regardless of which channel you watch.

How Climate is Framed

Climate news is not only about how often climate change is mentioned. It is also about how the issue is presented. Media frames shape which parts of the climate issue become most visible to audiences, such as costs, political decisions, or scientific evidence.

For now, we focus on three main frames:

Economy

This frame presents climate issues through economic effects. It includes coverage about costs, jobs, prices, industry, business interests, or the financial impact of climate policy.

Policy

This frame focuses on political decisions and regulation. It includes coverage about government plans, climate laws, public debate, elections, or conflicts between policymakers and affected groups.

Science / Expertise

This frame uses expert knowledge to explain climate change and its consequences. It includes coverage where scientists, researchers, or specialists explain climate impacts, causes, risks, or possible solutions.

By identifying these frames, the dashboard shows not only how much climate coverage appears in Flemish media, but also which angle is used to explain it.

Common Misinformation Scenarios

Although over the span of around 4 months no specific misinformation cases were found in the Flemish media, it is still crucial to take into account the existence of false information in the discourse around climate change.


For that purpose, a tool is added beneath wherein key misinformation narratives previously studied by Science Feedback in the French media have been added. In this case, being aware of these general narratives can better deepen your specific knowledge to recognise and reflect on misleading claims in the Flemish media sphere.
