Environmental Crisis Coverage

This chart shows the percentage of airtime dedicated to environmental crises in Dutch-language Belgian media. The red line represents total environmental crisis coverage, while the other lines separate climate, biodiversity, and resource-related crises. This helps identify when environmental issues became more or less visible in the media agenda.

Observation

"Research shows that peaks in environmental coverage often coincide with major climate events."

Media Coverage per Channel

These two charts zoom in on which Dutch-language Belgian channels drive environmental coverage. The left chart ranks channels by their average share of environmental airtime, broken down into climate, biodiversity, and resource-related coverage. The right chart adds a time dimension, showing how each channel's coverage fluctuates across the period — revealing whether attention is consistent or event-driven.

Observation

VTM appears to have the highest total environmental coverage among the selected channels, followed by Canvas and VRT 1. Radio channels such as STU BRU and QMUSIC show much lower levels of environmental coverage. This suggests that audiences may encounter very different levels of climate-related information depending on which outlet they follow.

Observation

Some channels show sharp peaks rather than steady coverage. Canvas is especially high in early February, while VTM and Play show major peaks later in the period. This suggests that environmental coverage is often event-driven and may depend on specific news moments rather than continuous attention.

Keyword Intensity

This chart shows the number of keyword hits over time for each crisis type. It allows us to see which environmental crises generate the most language and attention in Dutch-language media. Higher keyword counts indicate moments where a topic became more prominent in the news discourse.

Observation

"Climate crisis keywords dominate the chart, with several large spikes. Biodiversity-related keywords are more stable over time, while resource crisis keywords remain lower but still follow similar peaks. This suggests that climate is the most visible environmental crisis in the selected media data."

Keyword Count

These two visualisations show how environmental language appears across Dutch-language Belgian media. The keyword count highlights the most frequently used environmental terms overall, while the keyword-per-hour chart shows how strongly each channel uses this language over time. Together, they move from what words dominate the debate to which media outlets amplify those words most intensely during specific moments.

Observation

"Ecologisch," "vervuild," and "vergroenen" dominate the keyword ranking by a significant margin, suggesting that Dutch-language media frames environmental coverage primarily through broad ecological and pollution language rather than specific policy or crisis terms. Notably, words like "klimaatverandering" and "fossiele brandstoffen" appear far lower in the ranking — indicating that precise climate vocabulary is used far less frequently than general environmental language, which may limit the depth of public understanding conveyed through media coverage.

Observation

Environmental keyword usage is highly uneven across channels and over time. Canvas shows an exceptional spike in early February, reaching nearly double the rate of other channels at that moment, while STU BRU produces the sharpest single peak of the entire period in early May. Most channels hover at low baseline levels between events, reinforcing that environmental language in Dutch-language media is largely reactive — driven by specific news moments — rather than a consistent editorial priority.

Key Insight

70% of articles focus only on economic consequences, instead of ecological reality.

Sector Analysis

This chart shows how different sectors appear in environmental coverage over time. Sectors include energy, ecosystems, industry, agriculture, transport, buildings, water, and resource economy. This helps reveal which parts of society are most strongly connected to environmental reporting.

Observation

Energy dominates sector coverage across the entire period, with recurring spikes that suggest event-driven rather than sustained reporting. Ecosystem coverage remains consistently low despite its long-term ecological relevance, while sectors like agriculture and transport — which carry significant climate implications — receive minimal and sporadic attention. This pattern indicates a structural bias in Dutch-language media toward energy as the primary lens for environmental coverage, potentially at the expense of more systemic environmental storytelling.

Sector Analysis per Channel

This chart compares the sector composition of environmental coverage across Dutch-language channels. Each bar shows how a channel’s environmental coverage is divided across sectors such as energy, ecosystems, agriculture, industry, transport, buildings, water, and resource economy.

Observation

Energy dominates the sector composition across almost all channels, but its share varies considerably — VTM and Play dedicate a notably larger proportion to energy compared to radio channels. Ecosystem coverage is consistently present but remains a secondary topic everywhere. Agriculture, transport, and buildings appear as thin slivers across most channels, suggesting these sectors are structurally underrepresented in Dutch-language environmental media coverage regardless of outlet.
